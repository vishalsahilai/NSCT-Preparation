# NSCT HCI Study Strategy

A practical, repeatable method for learning each topic, choosing resources, practising questions and improving weak areas.

## 1. Follow one learning method for every topic

Use this sequence:

**Preview → Learn → Make Notes → Practise → Analyse → Revisit**

### Step 1 — Preview the topic
- Open the subject's `topics.md` file.
- Read the topic heading and subtopics.
- Identify prerequisite concepts.
- Mark what you already know and what is new.
- Break a large topic into smaller learning units.

### Step 2 — Learn the concept
Use the most suitable available resource:
1. Start with a clear beginner-level explanation or notes.
2. Watch a relevant video when it helps explain the concept.
3. Read official documentation or a trusted reference for technical details.
4. Work through at least one example yourself.

Do not collect many resources for the same topic before learning it. Choose one main resource, understand it, and use another only when something remains unclear.

### Step 3 — Make short notes
Write notes in your own words. Include:
- A simple explanation.
- Important terms and definitions.
- A diagram, formula or example when useful.
- Differences between easily confused concepts.
- One or two points you tend to forget.

Keep notes concise. The purpose is to help you revise, not to copy an entire video or article.

### Step 4 — Practise without help
- Start with basic questions to check understanding.
- Move to medium questions once the basics are clear.
- Attempt difficult or scenario-based questions after that.
- Do not reveal the answer before attempting the question.
- For programming and DSA, trace examples manually and write small programs where appropriate.
- For theory subjects, explain concepts in your own words and solve application-based questions.

Difficulty labels are a practice progression, not an official NSCT question classification unless confirmed by the exam authority.

### Step 5 — Analyse every answer
For each incorrect or guessed answer:
- Identify the concept being tested.
- Find the exact point you misunderstood.
- Explain why the correct answer is correct.
- Explain why your selected answer was incorrect.
- Write a short correction in your mistake log.
- Reattempt a similar question later.

A guessed correct answer should also be reviewed. A correct guess does not prove that the concept is understood.

### Step 6 — Revisit the topic
Review the topic after a gap rather than reading it repeatedly in one sitting.

A simple review pattern is:
- First review: later in the same study day.
- Second review: after one or two days.
- Third review: about a week later.
- Final review: during the relevant subject revision.

This is a suggested revision pattern. Adjust it when a topic remains difficult.

## 2. How to use learning resources

For each topic, follow this order:

| Resource type | How to use it |
|---|---|
| Repository notes | Use as your starting explanation and checklist. |
| YouTube videos | Watch the relevant section; pause and solve examples yourself. |
| Official documentation | Verify technical details and terminology. |
| Practice questions | Check whether you can apply the concept independently. |
| Your own notes | Use for quick revision and recall. |

### Resource quality checklist

Before relying on a resource, check:
- [ ] Does it cover the exact topic in the checklist?
- [ ] Is the explanation understandable?
- [ ] Are examples included?
- [ ] Is the content technically accurate?
- [ ] Is the resource accessible?
- [ ] Is it appropriate for the level of preparation?

A video link is a recommendation, not proof that the video fully covers the syllabus. If a link is broken or no longer relevant, find a suitable replacement and record the source.

## 3. How to practise MCQs

Use this progression for each topic:

### Level 1 — Easy
- Check definitions and basic understanding.
- Identify key terms.
- Solve direct questions.

**Move forward when:** You can explain the concept and answer basic questions without relying on notes.

### Level 2 — Medium
- Apply concepts to examples.
- Compare similar terms.
- Interpret short code snippets, diagrams, queries or scenarios where relevant.

**Move forward when:** You can explain your reasoning, not just select an option.

### Level 3 — Hard
- Solve multi-step or scenario-based questions.
- Combine concepts from related topics.
- Practise questions that require careful analysis.

**Move forward when:** You can explain the solution and identify why other options do not fit.

Do not use a single score as the only measure of readiness. Look at the types of mistakes you make and whether you can solve a similar question later.

## 4. Subject-specific practice methods

Use the subject's own `topics.md` checklist to decide what to study. These are practice methods, not additional claims about the official syllabus.

| Subject area | Useful way to practise |
|---|---|
| Problem Solving & Analytical Skills | Solve reasoning problems step by step; show your working. |
| Programming | Trace code, predict output and write small programs. |
| Data Structures & Algorithms | Draw structures, trace operations and compare approaches. |
| Databases | Practise interpreting schemas, queries and database concepts. |
| Software Engineering | Compare processes, models, testing concepts and scenarios. |
| Web Development | Trace how browser, client, server and web technologies interact. |
| Networks & Cloud Computing | Draw communication flows and compare protocols or service concepts. |
| AI / ML & Data Analytics | Explain terminology, workflows and use cases in your own words. |
| Operating Systems | Trace process, memory and scheduling examples where included. |
| Cyber Security | Match threats with vulnerabilities, controls and safe practices. |

## 5. Maintain a mistake log

Use a table like this in your notes or spreadsheet:

| Date | Subject | Topic | Mistake | Correct understanding | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| YYYY-MM-DD | Programming | Loops | Misread loop condition | Re-trace each iteration | YYYY-MM-DD |

Record:
- Incorrect answers.
- Correct answers that were guesses.
- Concepts you repeatedly forget.
- Questions you could not explain.
- Topics where you confused two similar terms.

Review this log during weekly revision.

---

## 6. Track progress honestly

Use `PROGRESS-TRACKER.md` to record topic status.

Suggested status meanings:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not Started | You have not studied the topic yet. |
| Learning | You are currently understanding it. |
| Needs Practice | You understand the explanation but need more questions or examples. |
| Reviewed | You have revisited the topic and can explain it. |

Do not mark a topic as reviewed just because you watched a video or read the notes. Try to explain it without looking and solve practice questions independently.

---

## 7. Weekly review method

At the end of each week:

1. Review the topics you completed.
2. Attempt a short mixed set from those topics.
3. Revisit incorrect and guessed answers.
4. Identify the three topics that need the most attention.
5. Schedule those topics into the next week's plan.
6. Update the progress tracker.

Use subject-wise tests and mixed practice to check both depth and recall.

---

## 8. Mock-test strategy

- First, understand the official test format from the official NSCT portal.
- Use mock tests that match the confirmed subject areas and format.
- If the official duration, number of questions or marking scheme is not confirmed, do not assume one. Label any self-imposed timing as practice.
- Attempt a mock without notes or outside help.
- After finishing, review every wrong and guessed answer.
- Categorise mistakes as: concept gap, misreading, calculation or reasoning error, coding error, or careless selection.
- Use the results to plan revision, not simply to compare scores.

---

## 9. Common mistakes to avoid

- Watching videos passively without solving examples.
- Collecting too many links instead of completing one resource.
- Memorising answers without understanding concepts.
- Skipping practice after reading a topic.
- Ignoring questions answered correctly by guessing.
- Studying only high-weight subjects and completely neglecting others.
- Marking topics complete without testing yourself.
- Taking mock-test scores as proof that every topic is mastered.
- Relying on unofficial exam claims instead of checking current official instructions.

---

## 10. Your repeatable topic checklist

Copy this checklist for each topic:

- [ ] I read the topic and its subtopics.
- [ ] I identified any prerequisite concepts.
- [ ] I studied a clear explanation.
- [ ] I used a relevant video or reference if needed.
- [ ] I wrote short notes in my own words.
- [ ] I worked through examples independently.
- [ ] I attempted easy questions.
- [ ] I attempted medium questions.
- [ ] I attempted difficult questions where available.
- [ ] I reviewed wrong and guessed answers.
- [ ] I added important mistakes to my log.
- [ ] I revisited the topic after a gap.
- [ ] I can explain the topic without looking at the notes.
