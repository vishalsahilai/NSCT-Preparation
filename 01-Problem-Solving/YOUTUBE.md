# YouTube Learning Resources: Problem Solving

Problem Solving and Analytical Skills are important parts of computer science preparation.

These videos can help you understand computational thinking, logical problem solving and the fundamentals of computer science.

## Recommended videos

### 1. CS50: Introduction to Computer Science

**Video:** [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8mAITcNt710)

- **Level:** Beginner to Intermediate
- **Focus:** Introduction to computer science and computational thinking.
- **What you will learn:**
  - How computers solve problems.
  - How to approach computational problems.
  - Basic algorithms and programming concepts.
  - How to think logically when solving problems.

**How to use it:**

Watch the introductory sections first. Focus on understanding how a problem is broken into smaller steps.

Do not try to memorise every programming example.

### 2. CS50 Introduction to Computer Science (2026)

**Video:** [Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gmuTjeQUbTM)

- **Level:** Beginner
- **Focus:** Computer science fundamentals and problem-solving concepts.
- **What you will learn:**
  - Basic computational thinking.
  - How programming relates to problem solving.
  - How to approach problems systematically.

**How to use it:**

Use this as an additional explanation when a concept from the first resource is difficult to understand.

## Topics to practise after watching

Use the subject's `topics.md` checklist as the source of truth.

Pay particular attention to:

- Logical reasoning.
- Analytical reasoning.
- Pattern recognition.
- Problem decomposition.
- Algorithms and step-by-step solutions.
- Flowcharts and pseudocode, where included in the checklist.

## Practice method

After watching a topic:

1. Read the corresponding topic in `topics.md`.
2. Solve a few examples yourself.
3. Write down the steps used to reach the answer.
4. Attempt related MCQs without looking at the answers.
5. Review mistakes and repeat difficult questions.

## Coverage note

These are introductory computer science resources. They do not necessarily cover every analytical reasoning or quantitative reasoning topic in the NSCT preparation checklist.

Use `topics.md` to identify any missing areas and find additional explanations when needed.
